# Observation 2026-09-15-59b0939f0c47

```text
The thumb tap for caret here is good, but vertically not in the middle of the text to which it refers.
```

<!-- triage -->

## Reading

Two separate things in one sentence, and they pull in opposite directions.

**The affirmation.** Placing the caret by thumb tap works on variant B, and he said so
unprompted. That is the half worth protecting: whatever variant B does to make the caret
thumb-tappable is a thing the next iteration must not lose. → `UX-001`.

**The defect.** Something is vertically off-centre against "the text to which it refers".
Whatever the referring element is, it should sit on the vertical centre of the line it
points at, not above or below it. → `UX-002`.

The document anchor contributes nothing here. The comment is attached to line 18 of
`mobile-ui/mobile-ui-review.md`, a heading that reads "## 1. Landing screen — the entry
page"; the comment is about the caret. This is the normal case, not an outlier.

## Uncertain

- **What "here" is.** The anchor records where the cursor was, not what was being looked
  at, so it does not identify the element. Variant B was running at
  `http://100.82.39.26:5175/` when this was written. Not resolved — ask before acting on
  `UX-002`.
- **What is off-centre.** "Vertically not in the middle" could describe the caret handle
  itself sitting off the vertical centre of its line, or the selection popup that appears
  above the tap. Both are consistent with the sentence. `UX-002` is deliberately worded to
  cover the general rule rather than to pick one, because picking one would be a guess.
